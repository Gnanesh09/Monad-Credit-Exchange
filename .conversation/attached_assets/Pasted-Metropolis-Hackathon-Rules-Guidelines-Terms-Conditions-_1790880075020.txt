3. HACKATHON OVERVIEW
3.1 Objective
Build and ship new products on the Monad blockchain across the four tracks described in Section 3.3, each supported by a working prototype, public source code, a demo video, and documentation.

3.2 Prize Structure
Total Prize Pool: $145,000 USD

Overall Winner ($25,000 total):

1 Overall Winner: $25,000 USD, awarded to the most standout project across all tracks
Track Prizes ($120,000 total):

$30,000 USD per track, across the four tracks described in Section 3.3
Within each track, $10,000 USD awarded to each of the top three projects
Additional Bounties:Sponsor-funded bounties may be added and announced during the Hackathon period, each with their own terms and requirements.

3.3 Tracks
The Hackathon is organized into four tracks. A submission may be entered in one track only.

Track 01 - Onchain Finance & Trading:financial instruments, markets, and asset primitives,
where the primary user is a trader, protocol, or financial product builder

Track 02 - Consumer Products & Payments:products whose primary user is a consumer who may not
identify as a crypto user, and whose core value is a financial experience rather than a trading or market making product

Track 03 - Social, Attention & Culture:products whose core user value is social connection,
cultural participation, or community, even where financial mechanics are involved

Track 04 - Trust, Identity & AI Infrastructure:protocols, primitives, or infrastructure layers
that other applications build on, rather than standalone consumer products

Every track requires a working product deployed on Monad, public source code on GitHub, a demo video, and documentation, in each case as described in Section 4.

For Sponsor bounties: the submission must meet the requirements published by the relevant Sponsor for that bounty

3.4 No Offer of Investment or Further Funding
Participation in the Hackathon does not entitle any Participant to an investment, grant, token allocation, accelerator place, or any other funding from the Organizer or any Sponsor. Any reference to venture support, mentorship, or downstream programmes describes opportunities that may become available and is not an offer, a commitment, or a solicitation. Any funding, if it occurs, would be made only under a separate written agreement and subject to the Organizer's own diligence and approval.

4. SUBMISSION REQUIREMENTS
4.1 Mandatory Requirements
All submissions must include:

1. Public GitHub Repositorycontaining:

Complete source code;
README with setup instructions;
Open source license (MIT, Apache 2.0, GPL, or similar);
Clear attribution of external code/libraries used; and
A commit history covering the build window.
2. Demo Video (3 minutes):

Publicly accessible (YouTube, Loom, Vimeo, etc.);
Clearly demonstrates the product in actual operation; and
Shows Monad blockchain integration.
3. Monad Integration:

Clear explanation of how the project leverages Monad;
Contract addresses (if applicable); and
Deployment to Monad mainnet or testnet.
4. Originality and Build Window:

The substantial majority of the work submitted must be created during the Hackathon period;
Pre-existing code or products may be used as a foundation only if the pre-existing components are
identified in the README and the submission adds substantial new functionality built during the Hackathon period; and

Use of AI coding tools is permitted and must be disclosed in the README.
5. Documentation:

Project description
Architecture overview
Technology stack
Setup/deployment instructions
6. Submission Platform:all submissions must be made through the Hackathon website. Use of the Hackathon website is subject to its own terms of use and privacy policy, which apply in addition to these Terms.
